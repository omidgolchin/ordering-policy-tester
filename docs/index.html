    The smoothing trade-off

    Order-up-to with smoothing closes only a fraction of the gap to your target each week. A smaller fraction calms your orders (a lower bullwhip ratio) but reacts slowly when demand moves, which can cost more.     How the verdict is decided

    The chain is simulated week by week for 36 weeks. Every stage starts with 12 units on hand and full pipelines, the classic opening of the Beer Game (Sterman, 1989). Each week a stage receives goods, gets its incoming order, ships what it can, pays holding cost on what is left and shortage cost on what it owes, then places its order. Deterministic demand patterns are simulated once; random demand is simulated over 20 demand paths and the verdict uses the median, with the middle 80% range shown.

    Order-up-to target. S = (average weekly demand × lead time) + z × (demand spread × √lead time), where z comes from the service level you pick (1.64 for 95%). It is the standard base-stock target (Silver, Pyke and Thomas, 2016). Without information sharing, each stage estimates demand from the orders it has received so far, which is exactly how the bullwhip starts (Lee, Padmanabhan and Whang, 1997).

    Bullwhip ratio. The variance of a stage's orders divided by the variance of customer demand (Chen, Drezner, Ryan and Simchi-Levi, 2000). 1.0 means orders are exactly as steady as demand; the classic result is that rational ordering rules still push it well above 1 at the factory.

    Smoothing. The proportional order-up-to rule orders a fraction of the gap between the target and the inventory position (Disney and Towill, 2003). The tool tries factors from 0.2 to 1.0 and keeps the cheapest at your stage.

    The anchoring bot. In Play mode, a teammate can use the anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic Sterman (1989) fitted to real players: expected demand updates by 0.36 of the surprise, and the order adds 0.26 of the gap to the target stock, counting only 0.34 of the supply line. It is the pattern real people fall into, and it whipsaws the chain.

    Costs, delays and the cost model come from the Compare settings on the left of that mode. A game lasts 36 weeks. Each week you see what arrived and what was ordered from you, then you place your order.
